About

Research roots, engineering practice

I hold a PhD in computer science from UC Davis, where my research was in data and scientific visualization, with around ten peer-reviewed publications — including a Best Paper award. That research background shapes how I work: I'm comfortable with the literature and with rigorous evaluation, and I care about getting ideas from a paper or a prototype all the way to a system people can actually use.

These days that bridge runs through ML infrastructure. On the training side — DeepSpeed and FSDP pipelines, multi-GPU comparisons, and post-training sweeps with crash recovery. On the agent side — multi-agent orchestration and self-learning training loops with hierarchical judges. For retrieval — RAG with hybrid search, reranking, and citation traceability. And because these systems are hard to debug, I build observability tracers and judge panels — plus interactive visualizations that turn ML concepts into something you can explore directly.
